One-Time Purchase vs Subscription: What AI Tool Pricing Hides From You

Kinet · pricing post · the author sells a one-time-purchase app, so treat this as a conflicted confession — we'll show you where buy-once is a scam too · 7 min read

"No subscription, ever" has become marketing catnip. As the developer of a buy-once AI tool, let me do the unfashionable thing: explain the actual economics of both models, including the part of buy-once pricing that vendors hope you never check.

Subscription fatigue is a data point, not a mood

Across consumer software, willingness to start yet another monthly plan keeps falling while the number of active subscriptions per household keeps rising. AI made it worse: now your tools want rent and your models want rent. A developer in 2026 can plausibly carry: a code-assistant sub, a chatbot sub, a notes-app sub, an image-gen sub — before a single API token is bought. The fatigue isn't irrational. It's a stacked-bill problem.

What a subscription actually prices

A sub is honest about one thing: it bundles hosted marginal cost — the vendor pays inference + storage + bandwidth per active user, so per-month pricing matches per-month cost. For genuinely hosted products (cloud compute, live sync infra, streaming APIs), the sub is the fair shape. No reasonable alternative exists.

What a one-time purchase actually prices

For local-first software, the marginal cost of one more user is ≈ $0. The binary sits on your machine; your API usage is billed by your own provider account. Pricing rent on it means charging twice for capacity the vendor never provisions. That's the whole argument.

But here's the part vendors don't volunteer: buy-once shifts a bet onto you. The vendor must fund future development from past sales. Weak vendors respond predictably:

  • abandonware — buy-once as an exit banner;
  • "v2 is a new product" — your license quietly doesn't cover the thing you actually use;
  • "free updates" with no stated window — technically true, meaningfully nothing.

So buy-once isn't automatically fairer. It's fairer when the terms are explicit. Which leads to the checklist.

Rule of thumb: subscription = paying for hosted marginal cost; buy-once = paying for development, once. Any vendor charging you both — rent and your own compute — is double-billing one of them.

When buy-once is the wrong answer

To be explicit: don't buy this model's promise where it doesn't hold. Continuous-hosting products (cloud sync heavyweights, live-API resellers, anything with servers keeping your state) cannot honestly be buy-once; a vendor claiming otherwise is pre-selling bankruptcy. Buy-once fits local-first tools: the value shipped is a binary that keeps working. If a "buy once" tool quietly requires the vendor's cloud to function, it's a sub wearing a costume — run it through the table above.
